Figure 5 · Which factor each thermometer listens to (H2b ablation)

News tracks unpreparedness, prices track the full product, stocks sit in between

Four intensity outcomes, each re-measured with three rulers (Spearman ρ within controlled commodities). Attention is driven almost entirely by pre-event unpreparedness — an information phenomenon: how loud the reversal is depends on how dark you were before. Commodity prices follow the full product, criticality × unpreparedness — a physical phenomenon: the supply must really be chokeable and nobody can have stockpiled. Stocks fall in between.
Unpreparedness only (1−V) Share only Product: criticality × unpreparedness dashed = not significant (p>0.05) · dotted † = marginal (p=0.051)
Data: attention = 30-day post-announcement news jump (targets n=22, corrected-term basis; diamond's post-announcement zero is a genuine reading, not a vocabulary artifact, so it re-enters the sample — pre-correction n=21 values were 0.82 / 0.12 / 0.53); stocks = CAR[0,+5] (n=24; share/product versions n=22, diamond has no share data); prices = offshore price change (one-month window n=10, longest window n=12). One-sided permutation p: attention 0.0005 / n.s. / 0.051 (marginal, not significant); CAR 0.004 / 0.10 / 0.009; long-window price 0.037 / 0.022 / 0.002; one-month price 0.21 / 0.004 / 0.0008.
Reading: the formula, awakening intensity = criticality × unpreparedness, passes direct validation in its home domain (prices) under the product specification (one-month ρ=0.89). The earlier "one-month window not significant (ρ=0.30)" came from mis-specifying a single-factor ruler; both results are reported. The specification was fixed by theory before the test was run (design doc v2.4, revision 2), not picked after seeing results. Share varies widely within controlled commodities (molybdenum 40% → heavy rare earths 99%) and carries the price explanation: tellurium and dysprosium were equally dark, but tellurium at 53% share rose 7% while dysprosium at 99% tripled.
