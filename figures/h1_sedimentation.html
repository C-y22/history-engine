Figure 1 · Sedimentation evidence (H1)

Gallium capacity rides the alumina wave, not the gallium price

China, 2000–2023. Primary gallium capacity never declines — through a 67% price collapse, producers idle output but no capacity exits. Capacity tracks the expansion of its host (alumina), in which gallium recovery is a byproduct circuit.
A · China alumina production — the hostmillion tonnes
B · China primary gallium capacity and reported production — the byproduct circuittonnes per year
C · Gallium price, single consistent basisUS$ per kg, low-grade (≤99.99%) avg. U.S. import unit value
Reading: if gallium capacity were priced on its own market, the 2011–16 collapse (C) should have forced exits; instead capacity held at 600 t while production flexed −61% (B) — the response came in flows, never in the stock. Capacity steps up with each alumina build-out wave (A): sunk capital sediments and does not retreat.
Sources: capacity & price — USGS Minerals Yearbook, gallium chapters 2002–2022, and Mineral Commodity Summaries 2016–2024 (latest revisions); production — USGS MYB reported figures (Asian Metal/CNIA basis); 2005 single point 18 t from Antaike/CNIA via Li et al. (2022); alumina — USGS MYB bauxite & alumina chapters. No China production figures published 2000–04 and 2006–10; gaps are left blank, not interpolated.
Basis notes: price is one consistent series (low-grade ≤99.99% avg. U.S. import unit value) for 2002–23; the 2000–01 producer price ($640/kg, 99.9999% basis) is excluded as not comparable. Capacity 2000–01 (hollow points; 10→100 t) is approximate design capacity from a 2002 Dalian Univ. of Technology thesis and is not comparable with the USGS operating-capacity series that begins 2003.
